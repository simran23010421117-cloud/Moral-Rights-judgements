Moral rights of authors (s 57, Copyright Act, 1957) in the Delhi High Court

Judgments coded: 4 · Window: 1 Sep 2021 – 31 Aug 2026 · Simran Pawar, Simran Manchanda, Sargam Gandotra, Medha Yugshree (Section B, SLS Nagpur) · data.csv · data.xlsx

Small-sample warning: with 4 judgments, everything below describes this dataset, not the Delhi High Court's overall approach to s 57.

1. Judgments per year

Two of the four judgments are from 2023 and one each from 2025 and 2026; none are coded for 2021, 2022 or 2024. The gap year 2024 is a collection gap to check, not evidence that the court was silent.

2. Outcome

Two judgments were dismissals (one an application to reject a plaint, one a final suit), one was a consent decree coded "partly allowed", and one ad-interim order is coded "Other" because it decided no final relief.

3. Who brought the case and who won

Corporate parties won both cases that produced a clear winner. Two of four are coded "Mixed or unclear" because they ended by consent or on interim terms.

4. Doctrinal trends

From settlement to gatekeeping. The 2023 matters (consent decree; ad-interim order) did not resolve any moral-rights question; the court recorded agreed terms or deferred the issue. The 2025 and 2026 matters are the only two that engage s 57 in reasoning, and they point in different directions.

2025, Envitech. At the Order VII Rule 11 stage the court treated s 57 as a right that exists with the author regardless of ownership, relying on Amar Nath Sehgal. Pleaded removal of authorship therefore gave a cause of action even though the copyright-ownership question (government work, s 17(d)) was left open.

2026, Gaurav Garg. After a full trial the court held the plaintiff was an employee, applied s 17(c), found he had not proved authorship, and so denied s 57 relief. Here s 57 depended on first proving authorship.

Tension to note. One decision keeps s 57 alive without settling ownership; the other makes it fail because authorship was not established. These are different procedural stages, so they need not conflict, but together they suggest the real battleground is proof of authorship and employment status, not the content of the moral right itself.

5. The judgments

Aude Priya Donatelle Wacziarg EP Engel & Ors v. Pan Macmillan Publishing India Private Limited & Ors

CS(COMM) 568/2020 And I.As.12618-20/2020, 2691/2021 · 15-09-2023 · coded by Simran Manchanda

Issue. Whether the publication and circulation of "A Remarkable Friendship", including alleged defamatory statements and reproduction of private letters, violated the plaintiff's right to copyright, privacy, reputation etc.

Held. The Suit was decreed by consent. The book could be published with agreed deletions, specified copyright related reliefs were granted, and certain claims were not pressed

St+Art India Foundation and Another v. Acko General Insurance

CS(COMM) 822/2023 And I.As. 22638/2023, 22639/2023, 22640/2023, 22641/2023, 22642/2023 & 22643/2023 · 10-11-2023 · coded by Medha Yugshree

Issue. Whether Acko's commercial use of the mural Humanity in advertisements infringed copyright or qualified for statutory exception concerning artistic works in public places

Held. The court directed Acko to remove online posts displaying the mural within 72 hours, while leaving the substantive question of copyright infringement and fair dealing open for adjudication.

Source copy

Envitech Consultants India Pvt. Ltd. v. Rudrabhishek Enterprises Limited & Ors

CS(COMM) 892/2023, I.A. 25294/2023, I.A. 31835/2024 & I.A. 32699/2024 · 24-11-2025 · coded by Sargam Gandotra

Issue. Whether the plaintiff's copyright infringement suit over detailed project reports could be rejected at the threshold on the grounds tha the work belonged to the governement and that the damages claim was undervalued

Held. The defendant's application for rejection of plaint was dismissed because ownership questions required adjudication and examination on merits and the plaint disclosed a cause of action.

Source copy

GAURAV GARG v. ALY MORANI & Ors

CS (Comm.) No. 200 of 2018 · 09-01-2026 · coded by Simran Pawar

Issue. Wheter the plaintiff, who claimed to have conceptualised the IPL Awards event, was entitled to authorship attribution, protection of moral rights and other reliefs despite the defendants' assertion that the the work was created during his employment

Held. The suit was dismissed as entitlement to relief under Section 57 of copyright act or proof of infringement was insufficient. The Plaintiff was held not to be owner of copyright due to insufficient evidence and unreliable documents and as per Sec. 17 ( c) of the Act.

Source copy

7. Limits

The sample is below the 10–20 judgments the assignment asks for, and was gathered from one database. Two of the four coded judgments (Wacziarg, St+Art) do not decide any s 57 question. Percentages and "trends" from four cases should not be generalised.
