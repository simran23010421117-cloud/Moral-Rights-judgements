##Topic
-Section 57 of the Copyright Act, 1957 gives an author of original work "special rights" that survive even after copyright is assigned. These rights are "Right to paternity" and "right to integrity". We were interested in this topic as moral rights is grey area where lawfully the ownership of copyright is in somebody else's hands but creativity and innovation of the original author is still protected and Delhi High Court hears the most judgements on IP disputes.
##how we found the judgements
-The judgements were found using SCC online, using the find by section feature. Section 57 of of the Copyright Act, 1957 was selected and the judgements from 1 September 2021 to 31 august 2026 were selected from the Delhi High Court were chosen. The total amount of judgements satisfying this criteria were four.

What surprised us is that the only individual author in the set lost on the merits, because the court held he was an employee and applied s 17(c) before reaching the moral right. The other two cases ended by consent or on interim terms, so they are coded "Mixed or unclear".
##limits 
-Four judgments cannot show how the Delhi High Court generally approaches s 57. Only two of the four (Envitech and Gaurav Garg) reason about s 57; in the other two the section appears only in the pleadings or prayers. Nothing here shows a trend over time, and the 2021 to 2022 and 2024 gaps may reflect our search, not the court
